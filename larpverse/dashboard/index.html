How to read this map

Three drones search a 3×3 grid of rubble piles. They land, listen for people, and use radar to look for breathing under the rubble. Nothing on this map is a confirmed person: it tells a ground team where to search first.

  • Pile circle (A1…C3, columns west→east, rows north→south). Solid ring = heard or visited; dashed ring = nothing heard, not cleared. The pulsing ring is the pile the drones are working now.
  • 64
    Acoustic priority 0–100: how urgently the acoustic agent wants a pile searched, from what the drones heard. It orders the search; it is not proof.
  • PING
    Radar found a breathing-like return at this pile.
  • LEAD
    Acoustic lead: a human-like sound (voice, knocking) was heard from this pile.
  • NON-HUMAN
    Only a dog, engine or debris was heard. Dismissed as a lead, but not cleared.
  • NOT CLEARED
    Nothing heard. Silence is not absence.
  • Radar ping: red dot, pulsing, with its 95% region. Its belief share is the share of this pile's radar belief at that spot, not the chance that someone is there. Click it for the 3D LiDAR view.
  • Insufficient evidence: hollow orange, dashed. Too few radar ranges agree (e.g. 2 of 3): rescan before acting on it.
  • Yellow cone: direction a landed drone heard a human-like sound from (its apex is the drone). Grey short cones are non-human sounds. Pick a listen in the Acoustic agent panel to see its cones.
  • Drone (white; yellow while listening with rotors off). green squares are landing sites the drones chose from the LiDAR map.
  • Dispatch: where the coordinator sent the fleet next, and why (see Dispatch decisions).
  • Staging: the operator's console and the drone pads.

Depth is shown as (nominal) when it is an assumed 1.5 m rather than measured, and as ± (radar estimate) when the radar measured it.
