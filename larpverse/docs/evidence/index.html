Larpverse · evidence brief · Weiguan Jinlong, Tainan 2016 case study

Silent survivors are the ones today's search can't find.

Rubble search still depends on the victim making a sound. Larpverse sends heavy drones that map the pile, call out, and lower a radar pod onto the rubble to detect breathing, so people who can't knock or shout get on the dig list too.

What Larpverse adds, and what we've measured

Finds people who can't make a sound

A 4.9 kg radar pod is winched onto the rubble and reads breathing up to 2.9 m beneath it in a 45 s scan. It reports a condition hint, such as "irregular breathing with pauses — check first", and rejects debris, animals and machinery.

D synthetic radar generator, real DSP; validated on line-of-sight real data only (6/6 breath holds, rate within 0.5/min)

Triage in the first half hour

In simulation on the Weiguan-scale site, the drones put 53% of trapped people inside a flagged search sector within 30 minutes of launch (median 8 min), and 83% within an hour.

D 10 simulated missions, docs/IMPACT_MODEL.md

Gets phones and voices connected

Each drone runs an open rescue Wi-Fi. A trapped person's phone can tap "I'm trapped" in six languages, and the speaker calls out "knock three times". Both feed the same ranking.

docs/HOTSPOT.md, docs/SPEAKER.md

Where the system stands today: on the small 3×3 grid it pings 12 of 40 victims across 10 seeds, with zero false pings. On the full Weiguan-scale map the pod currently pings none (0 of 170). A placement fix for that site is in progress, and the impact numbers below will be recomputed when it lands.

The slowest step is regulation. With aviation-authority consent, government agencies can already fly disaster missions at night, beyond sight and over people. The 0.4–2 GHz radar pod needs special approval from Taiwan's telecoms regulator, which only allows ultra-wideband at 4–9 GHz and bans it on aircraft. The plan is for the pod to transmit only while seated on the rubble. Allow 12–18 months.

!

What we do and don't claim

  • A drone-lowered breathing radar on a real pile fills a gap no deployed system covers.
  • It ranks where to dig sooner, including people who make no sound.
  • A drone-found candidate gets the INSARAG "V" mark (victim suspected), never "L" (live victim confirmed).
  • We never say an area is "cleared". A searched zone without a ping is "searched, not cleared".
  • We don't claim faster extrication. Digging people out is still the bottleneck.
  • The radar results are simulation on a synthetic generator. Real-world validation so far is line-of-sight only.
