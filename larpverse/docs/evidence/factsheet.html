Finding the survivors who can't call for help

Rubble search still waits for a knock or a shout. Larpverse sends heavy drones that map the pile, call out, and land on the rubble and scan it with a breathing-detection radar, so unconscious and silent people get on the dig list in the first hour.

The pattern repeats: Surfside 2021, where a woman's voice was heard on day 1 but she couldn't be reached; Rana Plaza 2013, where a woman found alive after 100 h died when a fire ended 20 h of tunnelling; Hualien 2018, where the search stopped at 106 h with the last victims under a load-bearing beam. People who can be heard get found. People who can't, often don't.

L?What Larpverse does

The loop, fully autonomous

  • Recon: three heavy-lift drones map the ruin with 128-channel LiDAR. Satellite imagery is stale after a quake.
  • Listen and call out: drones perch with rotors off. A loudspeaker asks "if you can hear me, knock three times" in Mandarin, Taiwanese Hokkien and English.
  • Land and scan: each drone lands on a flat spot on the pile over a lead, and its downward-facing radar reads breathing through the rubble. It gives a condition hint, such as "irregular breathing with pauses — check first".
  • Rescue Wi-Fi: every drone serves an open hotspot. A trapped person's phone can tap "I'm trapped" in six languages.
  • AI decides, code verifies: an agent ranks the dig order. Rules block unsafe or overconfident wording, and a deterministic fallback runs if the AI is unavailable.

Why a drone-carried radar is the gap

Breathing radar already works on rubble when a person carries it there. NASA/DHS FINDER found two heartbeats under each of two collapsed buildings in Nepal 2015, and 4 men were dug out. It's lab-rated through 9 m of rubble or 6 m of solid concrete. GSSI LifeLocator units (about 270 MHz) were used at Sichuan 2008 and Haiti 2010.

Today a rescuer carries these units onto an unstable pile, one spot at a time. Larpverse gives that class of radar wings and an autonomous planner: each drone carries a FINDER- or LifeLocator-class sensor and lands it on the pile.

!What we claim, and what we don't

  • A drone that lands a breathing radar on the pile fills a gap that no deployed system covers.
  • It ranks where to dig sooner, including people who make no sound.
  • Drone finds get the INSARAG "V" (victim suspected) mark for rescuers to confirm.
  • Never "cleared": a searched zone without a ping is "searched, not cleared".
  • No faster digging: extrication stays with rescue crews.
  • The radar results are simulation; real validation so far is line-of-sight.
