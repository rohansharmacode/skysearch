Case study: Weiguan Jinlong building, Tainan

  • On 6 February 2016 at 03:57 local time, a magnitude 6.4 earthquake centred in Meinong District, Kaohsiung, struck southern Taiwan.
  • The Weiguan Jinlong building (維冠金龍大樓) in Yongkang District, Tainan, was a residential complex built in the early 1990s: 16 storeys plus a basement, in nine blocks (A–I) arranged in a U shape facing west.
  • In the 6 Feb 2016 Meinong earthquake the whole building toppled from west to east across Yongda Road Section 2; 115 people died there. The search was declared over on 13 Feb, about a week later.
  • In 2019 Taiwan's Supreme Court upheld five-year sentences for the developer, two architects, a designer and a structural engineer for homicide through professional negligence.
  • Sources: zh.wikipedia 維冠金龍大樓; Taipei Times, 22 Feb 2019.

How to read this map

Three drones search a 3×3 grid of rubble piles. They land, listen for people, and use radar to look for breathing under the rubble. Nothing on this map is a confirmed person: it tells a ground team where to search first.

  • Pile circle (A1…C3, columns west→east, rows north→south). Solid ring = heard or visited; dashed ring = nothing heard, not cleared. The pulsing ring is the pile the drones are working now.
  • 64
    Acoustic priority 0–100: how urgently the acoustic agent wants a pile searched, from what the drones heard. It orders the search; it is not proof.
  • PING
    Radar found a breathing-like return at this pile.
  • LEAD
    Acoustic lead: a human-like sound (voice, knocking) was heard from this pile.
  • NON-HUMAN
    Non-human sound · not cleared: only a dog, engine or debris was heard. Dismissed as an acoustic lead, but the pile is not cleared.
  • NOT CLEARED
    Nothing heard. Silence is not absence.
  • Radar ping: red dot, pulsing, with its 95% region. Its belief share is the share of this pile's radar belief at that spot, not the chance that someone is there. Click it for the 3D LiDAR view.
  • Triage ring around a ping (dig order, from the breathing condition): double = immediate, thick = immediate · check, thin pair = urgent (check responsiveness), dashed = delayed, dotted = assess (no usable pattern). #1 is dug first. An aid for rescuers, not a diagnosis.
  • Hatched part of a searched pile: beyond radar reach from the sites the drones scanned from (≈3 m of rubble). Only acoustic leads cover it: not cleared.
  • Insufficient evidence: hollow orange, dashed. Too few radar ranges agree (e.g. 2 of 3): rescan before acting on it.
  • Yellow cone: direction a landed drone heard a human-like sound from (its apex is the drone). Grey short cones are non-human sounds. Pick a listen in the Acoustic agent panel to see its cones.
  • Drone (white; hollow with a small "ear" ring while landed and listening, rotors off). green squares are landing sites the drones chose from the LiDAR map.
  • Dispatch: where the coordinator sent the fleet next, and why (see Dispatch decisions).
  • Search zones the operator tasked (✎ Plan search): white outline. Dashed = not searched yet; grey hatch = searched — not cleared; red = ping inside; teal = acoustic lead. Proposed zones from recon are dashed with a "proposed" tag until you accept them; suggested circles are sounds heard outside every zone.
  • Blue = rescue Wi-Fi: each drone's open hotspot (faint disc), the mesh links back to the GCS (thin lines, Mbps), and phones that joined it with their RSSI candidate region (dashed circle). A ringed, brighter phone says it is trapped. Consent-based: no passive phone sniffing.
  • Guidance arrow (white, dashed): the route the loudspeaker read to a person heard on open ground, around the rubble, to the rescue tent. A speaker icon marks a drone that is playing.
  • Staging: the operator's console and the drone pads.
  • N
    Basemap: by default the drones' LiDAR relief of the (synthetic) rubble. Map and Satellite show the real case-study site (OpenStreetMap or imagery, dimmed) with the site frame anchored at the building (the N arrow, top right, shows true north). The pile layout is synthetic: nothing here marks where real victims were. Without network the map falls back to the LiDAR relief.

Depth is shown as (nominal) when it is an assumed 1.5 m rather than measured, and as ± (radar estimate) when the radar measured it.
