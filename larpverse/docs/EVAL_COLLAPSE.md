# Collapse-site evaluation: dispatch policies on the Weiguan reconstruction (30 seeds)

Generated by `python -m isaac.eval_collapse --report` on 2026-11-07. Site: the 320 m collapse-site reconstruction (`--site collapse`, 8 search sectors Z1-Z8 in the 160 m core, staging on the south street). Seeds 1-30; radar `dsp` (synthetic IF through the real DSP chain); acoustic `stub` (**synthetic clips generated from the hidden truth**, cleaner than the real acoustic pipeline: treat the acoustic policy's numbers as an upper bound); the mission's default scan budget, 3 drones, battery swaps at the pads. Brackets are bootstrap 95% CIs over seeds.

Policies: **acoustic** (ours), **nearest** (nearest unvisited sector, radar only), **raster** (serpentine Z8 Z2 Z4 Z6 Z5 Z3 Z1 Z7 from the staging street, radar only). All start at the same sector.

**The trapped people, sounds and radar returns are invented.** The geometry is a reconstruction of the 2016 Weiguan Jinlong collapse site; nothing here describes where anyone actually was. See `docs/CASE_STUDY_WEIGUAN.md` for how this relates to the real 2016 response.

## How to read this

- A ping is a firm radar `candidate`, matched to the nearest hidden person; within 3 m it counts. Pings are candidate search regions, never confirmed people.
- The acoustic policy only visits sectors it heard something human-like from; the others stay "not cleared" for ground teams. The baselines search until the scan budget runs out.
- Small N: differences inside the CIs are not evidence.
