# Grid evaluation: dispatch policies (20 seeds)

Generated by `python -m sim.evaluate_grid --report` on 2026-11-07. Seeds 1-20; radar `dsp` (synthetic IF through the real DSP chain); acoustic `stub` (**synthetic clips generated from the hidden truth**, cleaner than the real acoustic pipeline: treat the acoustic policy's numbers as an upper bound); mission budget 24 radar scans, 3 drones, battery reserve 25% with 60 s swaps at the pads, 8 scans max per pile visit, 3600 s limit. Brackets are bootstrap 95% CIs over seeds.

Policies: **acoustic** (ours: listen after each pile, go to the highest acoustic priority, revisit open leads), **nearest** (nearest unvisited pile, radar only, no listening), **raster** (fixed lawnmower A1 B1 C1 C2 B2 A2 A3 B3 C3, radar only). All start at the same pile.

## How to read this

- A ping is a firm radar `candidate` (3 strong agreeing ranges, single dominant mode), matched to the nearest hidden victim; within 3 m it counts, otherwise it is a false ping. Pings are candidate search regions, never confirmed people.
- Victims sit at voice / weak-voice / knock piles plus the scenario's reported start pile. The acoustic policy only visits piles it heard something human-like from, so it spends less time and fewer scans; the baselines search until the scan budget runs out and can find victims the acoustic agent missed.
- The acoustic policy stops when no lead at or above priority 20 is left; remaining piles stay "not cleared" for ground teams.
- Everything is synthetic (terrain, radar returns, here the acoustic clips). The DSP extractor is validated on real breathing recordings; the through-rubble loss is a model (docs/RADAR_PENETRATION.md). Small N: differences inside the CIs are not evidence.
